## 1. Why this offer works for this audience

Solar businesses already spend money on lead generation (that's literally what SolarLeadAds sells them). Their #1 complaint is always the same: **leads go cold because nobody replies fast enough.** A solar quote request that isn't answered within 5 minutes has a dramatically lower chance of converting — homeowners are usually collecting 3-4 quotes at once.

That is your hook. You are not selling "an AI chatbot." You are selling: **"stop losing the leads you already paid for."** This also makes it a natural upsell to any existing SolarLeadAds client, and a strong stand-alone offer to solar companies who do their own marketing.

Chatbot use cases to mention (pick 2-3 per message, don't list all of them — over-listing kills cold messages):
- Instant WhatsApp/website reply to quote requests, 24/7
- Pre-qualifies leads (roof type, bill size, homeowner vs renter, postcode) before a human touches them
- Books site-visit/survey appointments straight into their calendar
- Answers FAQs (finance options, warranty, install time) so sales reps stop repeating themselves
- Follows up automatically with leads who went quiet

## 4. How to build the lead list

### Method A — Google Maps (primary, do this first)
1. Search the terms in Section 2 for each target city (e.g. `solar panel installers Brisbane`).
2. For each result, open the listing and capture: business name, website, phone, WhatsApp (often same as phone), and city.
3. Visit the website — get a real name if possible (Owner/Director/Sales Manager on an About page) and the general inquiry email. If no name, use a role-based greeting ("Hi team").
4. Only add businesses that: have a working website OR active Google/Facebook profile, have at least a few reviews (proof they're operating, not dormant), and don't already have a visible chatbot on their site (check the bottom-right corner — if they already have one, skip or pitch an upgrade instead).
5. Log everything in a simple spreadsheet: Name | Company | City | Website | Email | WhatsApp | Segment | Country | Outreach Status | Date Contacted | Follow-up Date | Reply Notes.

### Method B — supplementary sources (use once Google Maps is exhausted per city)
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator** — search "solar" + "installer/director/owner" filtered by country; LinkedIn DMs also work as a soft third channel.
- **Trade associations & directories** — e.g. Clean Energy Council installer directory (Australia), MCS Certified installer database (UK), SEIA member directory / EnergySage installer network (USA). These are pre-vetted, higher-quality lists.
- **Facebook Business Pages & groups** — search "solar installer [city]"; local trade Facebook groups often list active small businesses.
- **Company registries** — Companies House (UK), ABN Lookup (Australia), state Secretary of State business search (USA) — useful to confirm a business is real and find a director's name for personalization.
- **Review platforms** — Trustpilot, Google Reviews, Yelp: businesses with recent reviews are active and worth prioritizing.

### List-building targets
- Aim for **150-200 contacts per city per week** across email + WhatsApp combined. Quality of personalization matters far more than raw volume — a 50-contact list where you actually visited each website beats a 500-contact scraped list every time.

## 5. Compliance — read this before sending anything

This is not legal advice; if you scale this significantly, get 30 minutes with a lawyer in each region. But here's the practical baseline:

| Region | Email rule | WhatsApp rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Australia** | Spam Act 2003: needs consent, but **"inferred consent" applies** when a business email is published on a company website/directory in a professional capacity, as long as your message is relevant to that business and you weren't told not to contact them. Always include your name, company, and an easy opt-out; honor opt-outs within 5 business days. | No dedicated law bans a one-off business WhatsApp message, but treat it the same way — identify yourself immediately, keep it low-volume, stop instantly if asked. |
| **UK** | PECR: **corporate subscribers (limited companies, LLPs)** can be emailed without prior consent. **Sole traders and partnerships count as individuals** and need soft opt-in or consent — so a one-person "Dave's Solar Cleaning" is treated more cautiously than "Solar Installs Ltd." Always identify yourself and give an opt-out. | Same practical approach as Australia — first message must be clearly identified, respectful, and easy to opt out of. |
| **USA** | CAN-SPAM Act: no consent required for B2B cold email, but you must use accurate sender info, a real subject line, a physical address (can be a registered business address), and a working opt-out honored promptly. | No CAN-SPAM equivalent for WhatsApp specifically, but WhatsApp's own Business Messaging Policy prohibits unsolicited bulk/automated messaging — see note below. |

**WhatsApp-specific risk:** WhatsApp's commerce policy is built around opt-in messaging, and sending identical templated messages to strangers in bulk from one number can get that number **flagged, rate-limited, or banned** — regardless of local law. Mitigate this by:
- Sending manually or in small batches (20-30/day per number to start), not through spam-style bulk tools.
- Personalizing the first line of every message (mention their company name, city, or a specific service they offer).
- Never sending the exact same message text to hundreds of numbers back-to-back — vary phrasing slightly.
- Considering the official **WhatsApp Business API** (via a provider like Twilio or 360dialog) once volume grows — it has proper opt-in flows and template approval, and protects your number.
- Treating any "STOP"/"not interested"/silence-after-one-follow-up as a hard no — remove from the list immediately.

**Universal rules regardless of country:** always say who you are and who you're with in the first line, always give a one-line way to opt out ("Reply STOP and I won't follow up again"), never buy or use a scraped consumer list, and keep records of who you contacted and when.

## 7. Cold WhatsApp Sequence (3-message sequence)

Keep WhatsApp shorter and more conversational than email — nobody wants a paragraph on WhatsApp. Send as **separate short messages**, not one wall of text, with a natural pause between them (a few seconds), like a real person typing.

### Message 1 — Day 0

```
Hi [First Name], I'm Leyla from SolarLeadAds 👋

Saw [Company Name] does solar [installs/cleaning/sales] in [City] — quick question, what happens to a quote request that comes in after hours or on a weekend right now?

We build AI chatbots for solar businesses that reply instantly, qualify the lead, and book the visit — even when your team's offline. Worth a quick look?
```

### Message 2 — Day 3 (follow-up if no reply)

```
Hey [First Name], just floating this back up in case it got lost 🙂

Happy to just send a 2-min demo of the bot in action for a solar business — no call needed, you can watch it whenever. Want me to send it over?
```

### Message 3 — Day 7 (last touch)

```
Last one from me, [First Name] — didn't want to keep messaging without a reason 😊

If catching after-hours leads isn't a priority right now, totally fine — just say "not now" and I'll leave it. If you ever want to see how it works, I'm here: hello@solarleadads.com

All the best with the season ahead!
```

**WhatsApp etiquette notes:**
- If they reply at all (even "not interested"), stop the sequence and respond like a human, don't send the next scripted message.
- Never message the same lead on both WhatsApp and email on the same day — pick one channel as primary per contact and use the other only as a backup after 2 unanswered touches.
- Use their actual first name and company name every time — generic "Hi there" messages get ignored or reported.

## 8. Adapting for other countries later (incl. if "UA" meant UAE)

If you do want to add the UAE (or another Gulf/EU market) later:
- UAE: WhatsApp is the dominant B2B channel there, often more effective than email — lead with WhatsApp, keep it relationship-toned, avoid hard-sell language in message 1.
- EU countries: GDPR + ePrivacy rules are stricter than UK/AU — get local legal guidance before mass cold-emailing individuals; B2B outreach to generic company inboxes (info@, sales@) is lower-risk than to named individuals.
- Always re-check each new country's spam law before launching — don't assume the UK/AU/USA rules above transfer directly.
